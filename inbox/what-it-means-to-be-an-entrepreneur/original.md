# What It Means to Be an Entrepreneur

Last week I had a call with a friend who works in Silicon Valley. He studied in Germany and later moved to the US. I took away a few lessons from that conversation that I want to write down here.

## 1. Distribution is the bottleneck

Today, building a product is easy. **Distribution is the hard part.** If you have a product, bring it out on a larger scale: work with influencers and run ads. Looking back, I didn't do this enough, and it cost me the chance to scale.

## 2. Mindset beats credentials

The people who succeed in Silicon Valley do so because of how they approach the world. My friend told me he learned nothing important at university and taught himself everything that mattered. To win, you need the mindset that you can **understand things by yourself**, and you shouldn't outsource everything to AI tools.

## 3. Think until it hurts

Think deeply about things and understand them fully. What does it actually mean? **Think until your brain hurts.** Question your surroundings, and question them hard.

## Know your numbers

One more lesson comes from my Technology Entrepreneurship class at Berkeley. It taught me that you should always know your numbers: if you take action X, what does it cost, and what does it bring in? Not everything has to be profitable at the beginning, but you should know where you stand.

My intuition for growth is decent, but numbers (that means spreadsheets) tell you far more precisely what works and what doesn't.
